How the model works

Plan. Walls are thick line segments, doors are gaps, and rooms are found by flood-filling between the walls, starting from your room labels. Each room type carries a weight: offices 1.5, living rooms 1.2, kitchens and bedrooms 1, baths 0.4, garages 0.2, closets almost nothing.

Radio. Log-distance path loss plus a loss for each wall face a straight path crosses: interior about 2 dB per face, bathroom 3, garage 3.5, exterior 5, and the floor between levels 14 dB at 5 GHz. Shallow angles cost up to 2.5 times more. 2.4 GHz loses less, 6 GHz more. These are typical published figures, not measurements of your home, so treat predictions as ±6 dB.

Antenna shape. A ceiling AP is a donut squashed downward by its metal back plate. It's strongest 30–60° below the ceiling, about 4 dB weaker at the horizon, and 10–14 dB weaker toward the floor above. That is why a ceiling AP barely helps the room right over it.

Scoring. Every sample point scores from 0 at −72 dBm to 1 at −55 dBm, averaged over 5 and 6 GHz, weighted by room type. The optimizer tries every combination of ceiling spots on a coarse grid, then polishes the winner on a 1 ft grid.

Phone spots. Each spot is a circle about the size of what you pick (chair, couch, bed or desk), sampled at the height you'd hold a phone there. All spots together carry the same weight as the whole house.
